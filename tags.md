# Audio tags (Eleven v4 / v3)

From ElevenLabs' prompting guide (elevenlabs.io/docs/best-practices/prompting).
ElevenLabs says tags are "not perfect yet" and that a tag "can occasionally be
interpreted as a request for a sound effect", so stick to the voice tags below
and listen to the take.

## Emotion and delivery (the useful ones for a voiceover)

`[excited]` `[curious]` `[sarcastic]` `[mischievously]` `[happy]`
`[professional]` `[sympathetic]` `[reassuring]` `[questioning]` `[cute]`

## Breath, laughs and pauses

`[laughs]` `[laughs harder]` `[starts laughing]` `[giggles]` `[sighs]`
`[exhales]` `[pauses]` `[whispers]`

## Leave these out of a voiceover

Sound effects (`[applause]`, `[clapping]`, `[gunshot]`, `[explosion]`,
`[swallows]`, `[gulps]`) and novelty tags (`[sings]`, `[woo]`, accents) produce
sounds, not delivery.

## How to place them

- One tag at the start of a beat. It colours what follows until the next tag.
- Match the tag to the line. `[excited]` on a disclaimer sounds unhinged.
- Do not stack: `[excited] [happy] [laughs]` is three instructions for one line.
- Short text plus a strong tag can go over the top. Give a tag a full sentence.
